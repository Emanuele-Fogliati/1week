For product managers and product owners

PM AI Toolkit

9 battle-tested templates and 20 AI prompts to go from raw customer notes to a sprint-ready backlog in hours, not weeks.

How to use this toolkit

The templates give structure; the prompts do the first draft. Your job is judgment: check every assumption, cut what doesn't matter, and decide.

Rules that make AI output good:

  • Always paste real context: notes, data, quotes. No context, generic output.
  • Ask the model to mark assumptions as [ASSUMPTION] and never to invent data.
  • Iterate: "make it shorter", "what's missing", "argue against this".
  • Never paste confidential company or customer data into tools your company hasn't approved.

Template 4

Customer interview script

45 minutes, problem-focused. Never pitch your idea during discovery.

Intro (3 min) Thanks, purpose, no right answers, permission to record.

Context (7 min)

  • Tell me about your role and a typical week.
  • Where does [problem area] fit in your work?

Story of the last time (20 min)

  • Tell me about the last time you [did the task / hit the problem]. Walk me through it step by step.
  • What did you try first? What happened?
  • What was the hardest part? Why?
  • What did it cost you (time, money, stress)?
  • What do you use today to deal with it? What do you like and dislike about it?

Wrap-up (10 min)

  • If you could wave a magic wand, what would change?
  • Is there anyone else I should talk to?

Rules: ask about the past, not the future · ask "why" and "tell me more" · no leading questions · write down exact words.

Template 6

Worked example: a complete PRD

A full PRD for a realistic feature, to show the level of detail that works.

Sample PRD: Smart appointment reminders

  • Worked example. The product CareSlot is fictional; all numbers are illustrative assumptions, labeled as such.

Author: Product team · Status: Draft for review · Last updated: 2026-11-06

1. Problem

CareSlot is a booking app for small physiotherapy and dental clinics (1-10 practitioners). Clinics tell us no-shows are their biggest avoidable loss: an empty 45-minute slot cannot be resold at short notice.

Today CareSlot sends one fixed email 24h before the appointment. Patients often miss it, and there is no easy way to cancel or reschedule from the reminder, so they simply don't show up.

Assumption to validate: no-show rate in our clinics is ~8-12% of appointments (industry figures vary; we will measure our own baseline first, see §8).

2. Goals and success metrics

GoalMetricTarget (90 days after launch)
Fewer no-showsNo-show rate per clinic-30% vs. baseline
Freed slots get reused% of cancelled slots rebooked before start≥ 25%
Clinics see the valueClinics that keep reminders enabled after 30 days≥ 85%

Guardrail: patient opt-out from reminders stays under 5%.

3. Users

  • Clinic owner / receptionist: wants a full calendar and zero manual phone calls.
  • Patient: wants a reminder at the right time, on the right channel, and a one-tap way to say "I can't make it".

4. Scope

In scope (v1)

  • Reminders via SMS and email, at times set by the clinic (default: 48h and 3h before).
  • One-tap Confirm / Cancel / Reschedule links in every reminder, no login needed.
  • When a patient cancels, the slot is reopened and offered to the clinic's waitlist.
  • Dashboard tile for the clinic: confirmed, cancelled, no-show this week.

Out of scope (v1)

  • WhatsApp channel (pending provider evaluation and cost).
  • Automatic no-show fees or deposits.
  • AI-predicted no-show risk (candidate for v2 once we have data).

5. Requirements

IDRequirementPriority
R1Clinic can enable/disable reminders and choose up to 2 send timesMust
R2Reminder contains date, time, practitioner, address, and Confirm/Cancel/Reschedule linksMust
R3Links work without login, are unique per appointment and expire at appointment startMust
R4Cancelling frees the slot within 1 minute and notifies the clinicMust
R5Freed slot is offered to waitlisted patients, first to accept gets itShould
R6Patient can opt out of SMS reminders; email remains as fallbackMust
R7Reminders respect quiet hours (no SMS 21:00-08:00 local time)Should

6. Example user stories

US-1 As a patient, I want to cancel from the reminder in one tap, so that I don't have to call the clinic.

  • Given I received a reminder for an upcoming appointment, when I tap "Cancel" and confirm, then the appointment is cancelled, I see a confirmation screen, and the clinic is notified.
  • Given the appointment has already started, when I open the link, then I see "This appointment can no longer be changed online" and the clinic's phone number.

US-2 As a receptionist, I want freed slots offered to the waitlist automatically, so that I don't have to phone patients one by one.

7. Risks and mitigations

RiskMitigation
SMS cost erodes margin for small clinicsSMS on paid plan only; email free; show cost estimate in settings
Reminder links forwarded to othersLinks only allow cancel/reschedule of that appointment, no personal data beyond what's in the message
GDPR: health-related data in SMSNo treatment type in reminder text, only "appointment at [clinic]"

8. Rollout and measurement

1. Two weeks measuring baseline no-show rate across pilot clinics (no feature change).

2. Pilot with 10 clinics, 4 weeks, compare to baseline.

3. General release if no-show reduction ≥ 15% and opt-out < 5%.

9. Open questions

  • Do clinics want to customise the reminder text, or does that create support load?
  • Should rescheduling show all free slots or only those of the same practitioner?

Discovery and research

Prompt 1

Turn raw notes into an interview summary

You are a senior product researcher. Here are my raw notes from a customer interview: [paste]. Summarise in: (1) context of the person, (2) the job they are trying to get done, (3) pains with exact quotes, (4) current workarounds, (5) surprises. Do not invent anything that is not in the notes; mark anything uncertain.
Prompt 2

Find patterns across interviews

Here are summaries of [N] customer interviews: [paste]. Group the pains into themes. For each theme give: how many interviews mention it, the strongest quote, and which customer segment it affects most. End with the 3 riskiest assumptions these interviews do NOT yet answer.
Prompt 3

Write a non-leading interview script

I want to understand how [user type] deal with [problem area]. Write a 45-minute interview script focused on past behaviour, not opinions about the future. Flag any question that could be leading and rewrite it.
Prompt 4

Competitor teardown

Act as a product strategist. For [competitor 1], [competitor 2], [competitor 3] in the [market] market, compare: target customer, core promise, pricing model, onboarding, key features, and the obvious gaps. Use only what I paste below from their websites: [paste]. Then list 3 positioning opportunities for us.
Prompt 5

Jobs-to-be-done statements

Based on this research [paste], write 5 jobs-to-be-done statements in the format 'When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]'. Rank them by how often they appear in the evidence.

Strategy and prioritisation

Prompt 6

Opportunity solution tree

Our desired outcome is [metric and target]. From these customer insights [paste], build an opportunity solution tree: outcome → 3-5 opportunities (customer needs) → 2-3 solution ideas each → one cheap experiment per idea.
Prompt 7

RICE scoring with reasoning

Score these initiatives with RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort): [list with any data I have]. Show your assumptions for each number, flag where confidence is low, and tell me which 2 scores would change the ranking if they were wrong.
Prompt 8

Pre-mortem

Imagine it is 6 months after launching [feature] and it failed. Write the 8 most likely reasons why, grouped into value, usability, feasibility and business viability risks. For each, suggest an early warning signal and a mitigation.
Prompt 9

Define a North Star metric

Our product is [description] for [users]. Propose 3 candidate North Star metrics. For each: why it reflects customer value, how it could be gamed, and 3 input metrics teams can influence directly.
Prompt 10

Say no to a stakeholder

A stakeholder asked for [request] because [reason]. It doesn't fit our current goal [goal]. Draft a short, respectful message that acknowledges the need, explains the trade-off with data, and offers an alternative or a date to revisit.

Specs and delivery

Prompt 11

PRD first draft from a brief

You are an experienced product manager. Write a PRD for [feature] using this structure: problem, goals and metrics (table), users, in/out of scope, requirements table with MoSCoW priority, risks, rollout, open questions. Context: [paste]. Mark every assumption as [ASSUMPTION] so I can verify it.
Prompt 12

Break an epic into user stories

Break this epic into vertical user stories that each deliver user value and fit in one sprint: [epic]. Use 'As a / I want / so that'. Add Given/When/Then acceptance criteria including at least one edge case and one error case per story.
Prompt 13

Find gaps in a spec

Review this spec as a sceptical senior engineer and a QA lead: [paste]. List missing edge cases, unclear requirements, untestable acceptance criteria, and questions the team will ask in refinement. Be specific.
Prompt 14

Write analytics events

For this feature [description], propose the tracking plan: event names (snake_case), when they fire, properties with types, and which product question each event answers. Keep it under 10 events.
Prompt 15

Estimate risk, not just effort

For each of these stories [paste], identify the biggest unknown (technical, design, or dependency) and suggest a spike or prototype that would reduce it in under 2 days.

Communication

Prompt 16

Stakeholder update

Turn these notes [paste] into a 150-word weekly update for leadership: headline status (on track / at risk / off track), what shipped, metric movement, top risk and what we need from them. No jargon.
Prompt 17

Release notes for users

Rewrite these internal changelog items [paste] as user-facing release notes. Lead each with the benefit, max 2 sentences each, friendly but not cheesy.
Prompt 18

Explain a trade-off

Explain to a non-technical audience why we chose [option A] over [option B] for [decision]. Include what we gain, what we give up, and when we would revisit. Max 200 words.
Prompt 19

Meeting to decisions

Here is a meeting transcript [paste]. Extract: decisions made, open questions with owner, action items with owner and due date. If a decision is ambiguous, list it under 'needs confirmation'.
Prompt 20

Customer email for a sunset

Draft an email telling customers we are retiring [feature] on [date]. Explain why in one sentence, what they can use instead, how to export their data, and where to get help. Empathetic and clear.
